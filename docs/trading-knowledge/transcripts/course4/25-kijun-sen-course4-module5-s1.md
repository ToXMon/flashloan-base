Kijun bounces or rejections are essentially mean reversion strategies for uptrends or downtrends. The key here is the word trend. Remember, Ichimoku only works in a trending environment. It is horribly inefficient in range-bound markets.

So once we get good at identifying market conditions, we can then use the Kijun as a guide for trading a trending market. Now, if we remember from course two that the Kijun is essentially the average of the high and low for the previous X amount of periods. Unironically, this translates into a dynamic 50% Fibonacci retracement level. This will make sense, too, if we think about it.

The Kijun is the mean reversion or 50% retracement of any given trend. As price makes a new high or a low, the Kijun-sen moves up or down to show the new mean of the trend. Now, since we know it's the 50% retracement, we can use it as a trigger for entering trending markets during a pullback. The important point here is that price always wants to return to the Kijun.

Since this is a dynamic 50% fib, whenever price is close to the Kijun, we can say the trend is in equilibrium. When price is far away from the Kijun, we can say that that trend is overextended. And now this knowledge is what provides us the actionable nuance. Having bids or asks on or around the Kijun are likely high probability entries into the market during a strong trend.
