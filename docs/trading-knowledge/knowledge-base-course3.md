## Key Concepts Glossary

- **ask**: The minimum price a seller accepts for an asset on an exchange orderbook.
- **bid**: The maximum price a buyer offers for an asset on an exchange orderbook.
- **breakout**: Price movement through a defined support or resistance level with increased volume.
- **candle**: Visual representation of price action showing open, high, low, and close for a time period.
- **consolidation**: Price moving sideways within a narrow range after a trend, indicating indecision before next move.
- **contract**: Agreement to buy or sell an asset at a predetermined price; in crypto, often refers to perpetual/futures contracts.
- **cross margin**: Margin mode where entire account balance is shared across all positions as collateral.
- **deposit**: Transferring funds (crypto or fiat) into an exchange account for trading.
- **entry**: The price point at which a trader opens a position.
- **exit**: The price point at which a trader closes a position.
- **funding rate**: Periodic payment between long and short traders in perpetual futures to anchor price to spot.
- **futures**: Financial contracts obligating buyer/seller to transact at a predetermined future date and price.
- **isolated margin**: Margin mode where only the allocated margin for a specific position is at risk.
- **leverage**: Borrowing capital to increase position size beyond account balance (e.g., 10x leverage = 10x exposure).
- **limit order**: Order to buy/sell at a specific price or better. Executes only when market reaches the specified price.
- **liquidation**: Forced closure of a leveraged position when margin falls below the maintenance requirement.
- **liquidity**: Ease of buying/selling an asset without significantly affecting its price.
- **long**: Buying an asset expecting its price to rise. Profit from upward movement.
- **margin**: Collateral deposited to open and maintain a leveraged position.
- **market order**: Order to buy/sell immediately at the best available current price.
- **order book**: Real-time list of buy (bid) and sell (ask) orders organized by price level.
- **perpetual**: Futures contract with no expiration date, common in crypto trading.
- **position**: Active trade exposure in the market (long or short).
- **position sizing**: Determining the appropriate amount of capital to risk on a single trade.
- **range**: Price moving between defined support and resistance levels without breaking out.
- **resistance**: Price level where selling pressure historically overcomes buying, preventing further upward movement.
- **risk management**: Strategies to protect capital: position sizing, stop losses, diversification.
- **short**: Selling an asset expecting its price to fall. Profit from downward movement.
- **slippage**: Difference between expected trade price and actual execution price.
- **spread**: Difference between the highest bid and lowest ask price on an orderbook.
- **stop loss**: Order to automatically close a position at a predetermined price to limit losses.
- **support**: Price level where buying pressure historically overcomes selling, preventing further decline.
- **take profit**: Order to automatically close a position at a predetermined price to secure gains.
- **trend**: General direction of price movement over time (uptrend, downtrend, sideways).
- **volume**: Total number of units traded in a given period, indicating market participation strength.
- **wick**: Thin line on a candlestick showing the high/low extremes reached during the period.
- **withdraw**: Transferring funds out of an exchange to an external wallet.

## Agent Trading Rules (Extracted from Course Content)

These rules are distilled from the course material for systematic trading agent implementation:

1. Always use stop losses. Never enter a position without a defined exit point for loss.
2. Risk no more than 1-2% of total account balance on any single trade.
3. Understand order types before trading: market orders for speed, limit orders for price control.
4. Read the orderbook before entering: check bid/ask depth, spread, and liquidity.
5. Start with low leverage (2-5x). Higher leverage amplifies both gains and losses.
6. Use isolated margin for individual positions to limit risk to allocated funds only.
7. Monitor liquidation price at all times when using leverage. Never let positions approach liquidation.
8. Identify consolidation zones before entering. Wait for breakout or rejection confirmation.
9. Trade with the trend when possible. Range trading requires precise entries at support/resistance.
10. Support and resistance levels are zones, not exact prices. Use wicks and volume to validate.
11. Build a written trading system: entry criteria, exit criteria, position sizing, risk rules.
12. Backtest your system before risking real capital. Paper trade to validate.
13. Record every trade in a journal: entry reason, exit reason, outcome, lessons learned.
14. Review your trading journal weekly. Look for patterns in wins and losses.
15. Meditation improves trading discipline. Practice daily to build emotional regulation.
16. Trading full-time requires 6-12 months of consistent profitability first.
17. Never revenge trade. If you hit your daily loss limit, stop trading for the day.
18. Position size down when on a losing streak. Size up only after consistent wins.
